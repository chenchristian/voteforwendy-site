Not Our Sons and Daughters. Not Our Children.

When my husband and I watched our two boys’ step into adulthood — navigate college, build friendships, figure out who they were going to be — we felt the quiet, enormous pride that every parent in the East Bay knows. We wanted them to build futures. Pursue their dreams. Thrive in a safe and prosperous country.

We did not raise them to be sent into a foreign war by politicians who have spent decades funding conflicts they cannot define, measuring success by metrics they will not publish, and sending other people’s children to pay the price for their decisions.

What I am about to tell you is documented, publicly available, and barely covered by the mainstream press: establishment politicians from both parties have been quietly laying the groundwork to revive military conscription in the United States. They did not call it a draft. They called it “automatic Selective Service registration” — utilizing federal databases to sweep 18-to-25-year-olds into the draft pool without their explicit consent. And when asked directly whether a future military draft is “off the table,” Washington insiders have repeatedly refused to say yes.

Let me be completely clear: a vote for me is a vote against the military draft. Full stop.

The All-Volunteer Military Is a National Treasure

The United States military is the most powerful, skilled, and dedicated volunteer fighting force in human history. The men and women who wear our uniform do so out of genuine patriotism and genuine choice. That is not an accident. It is the direct result of a decision made after Vietnam to build a military based on professional commitment rather than compelled service.

Forced conscription does not make the military more effective. It dilutes professional ranks with unwilling participants, creates enormous logistical and training burdens, and destroys the morale of a force built on voluntary commitment. And it sends the clearest possible signal about the nature of the conflict being fought: if a war requires conscription to sustain, it is a war the American people have not freely chosen to fight.

Any war worth fighting will find volunteers. If it won’t, that tells us everything we need to know about whether we should be fighting it.

What Is Actually Happening in Washington

Career politicians from both parties — insulated by wealth, by age, by status — are making decisions about military commitments with no accountability to the families who would bear the cost. They write blank checks for overseas conflicts. They maintain permanent military presences in regions with no defined American interest. And when those commitments eventually exceed the capacity of a volunteer force, the draft is what they reach for.

The moment Congress stops having to ask for volunteers is the moment Congress loses its most powerful incentive to end wars. Conscription is not just a policy. It is the mechanism that allows endless wars to continue without political consequence. I will not allow it.

My Platform Against Forced Conscription

  • Repeal and dismantle automatic Selective Service enrollment — I will co-sponsor and champion legislation to abolish automatic enrollment in the Selective Service and restore the requirement for explicit, voluntary registration. Young Americans should choose whether to make themselves available for military service — not be opted in by federal database without their knowledge or consent.
  • Oppose any legislation that enables or expands the draft — I will vote against any bill that creates a pathway to mandatory military service, expands the pool subject to conscription, or weakens the legal protections that currently require congressional action before any draft can be implemented.
  • Fight for a fully funded, fully equipped all-volunteer force — The answer to any military manpower challenge is not conscription. It is making voluntary service more attractive through competitive pay, better housing, comprehensive mental health support, and educational benefits that reflect the real sacrifice of service.
  • Restore congressional war powers — Congress must stop delegating the decision to go to war to the executive branch. If a military commitment is worth making, it is worth debating openly, authorizing legally, and funding transparently. The era of blank-check authorizations for endless conflict must end.

A Promise to the Families of CA-14

The college students in Alameda County — at Cal State East Bay, at Chabot, at Las Positas, at UC Berkeley — deserve to finish their degrees, build their careers, and choose their own paths. Their parents brought them into this world and raised them for exactly that.

I will not allow Washington to treat them as a reserve pool for wars that cannot attract volunteers. Not on my watch.
